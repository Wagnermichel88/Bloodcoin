Bloodcoin

A token whose issuance follows weekly mortality in the United States

The legal notice in section 15 applies to the whole document.

Abstract

Bloodcoin (ticker BCUS) is an ERC-20 token on Base whose weekly issuance is multiplied by a factor derived from the number of deaths in the United States published by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. A signed oracle writes each weekly figure to the ledger, where it stays as a permanent public record. Issuance follows a Bitcoin-like halving schedule with a hard cap of 21 million tokens. Half of each week's issuance goes to proof-of-work miners and half to stakers, so anyone can obtain BCUS without the founder's involvement. The founder allocation of one million tokens vests linearly over ten years. All contracts are immutable and have no owner. This paper specifies the mechanism, its parameters and its limits, and states openly where trust is still required.

1Introduction

Most cryptocurrencies issue new units on a schedule that ignores the world outside the network. Bitcoin halves its block reward every 210,000 blocks regardless of what happens elsewhere [1]. Elastic-supply tokens such as Ampleforth react to the outside world, but only to their own market price [4].

Bloodcoin connects issuance to a measurement that has nothing to do with markets: the weekly count of deaths in the United States. The figure is among the most carefully collected public statistics in the world, it is published openly, and it is revised in a documented way. Every week the count is written to the ledger and stays there. Over decades the chain becomes a chronicle of mortality that anyone can read and verify.

The project is first an artwork and a public record. It is also designed so that it can work as a currency that runs without its founder: no one owns the contracts, no one can change the rules, and new tokens reach the public through mining and staking rather than through sales.

3The mortality oracle

3.1Data

The oracle uses the weekly all-cause death counts for the United States from the CDC's public data portal [8]. Recent weeks are revised as late death certificates arrive, so the report only uses weeks that are at least eight weeks old.

3.2Target factor

Each report carries two numbers. D is the sum of deaths in the four most recent usable weeks. D0 is the baseline: the average of the same four calendar weeks in the three preceding years. The relative deviation in basis points is

δ = 10,000 · (D − D0) / D0(1)

Deviations of up to ±300 basis points (3 %) are ignored. Beyond that, each further percent of excess mortality lowers the target factor by five percent, down to half. Fewer deaths than the baseline never raise it: the target then stays at 1.0. A bonus for low mortality would mostly reward two errors: three years after a pandemic, its deaths sit in the baseline and an ordinary year looks like low mortality; and a CDC undercount would look the same. With factors written in thousandths:

t = max( 500 , 1000 − 5 · max( δ − 300 , 0 ) / 10 )(2)

3.3Step limit and checks

The factor f that the emission actually uses moves toward the target by at most 50 thousandths per report. A single report, correct or not, can therefore change weekly issuance by at most five percent, and it takes at least ten consecutive reports to move the factor from 1.0 to the lower limit. The contract rejects reports with D outside 80,000 to 1,000,000, and reports where D and D0 differ by more than a factor of two.

3.4Retirement

The oracle can be switched off permanently with a signed message. Retiring it resets the factor to exactly 1.0, so a stolen key cannot push the factor to an extreme and then freeze it there. After retirement the token keeps issuing on the plain halving schedule.

3.5Verification

Every report is published on the oracle page together with the raw CDC rows it was calculated from. A button on that page repeats the calculation in the reader's browser directly against the CDC portal. Anyone can therefore check each figure without trusting the publisher's server.

4Issuance schedule

Time is divided into weekly epochs n = 0, 1, 2, … starting at deployment. The base amount is B = 10,500,000 / 416 ≈ 25,240.38 BCUS per week, and it halves every 416 epochs, about every eight years. The amount minted for epoch n is

En = B · 2−⌊n / 416⌋ · fn / 1000(3)

where fn is the factor in force during that epoch. Minting stops for good once total supply reaches 21,000,000. A factor below 1.0 means fewer tokens in that week; the difference is never made up later.

5Mining

Half of every weekly mint goes to the BcusMining contract. Anyone can earn it with a computer and a Base address, without holding BCUS beforehand. The design follows the mineable-token pattern of EIP-918 [9] with two changes: the reward is paid by elapsed time rather than per solution, and difficulty retargets on a fixed solution count.

5.1Proof of work

A miner searches for a number k (the nonce) such that

keccak256( c ‖ a ‖ k ) ≤ T(4)

where c is the current challenge, a the miner's own address and T the current target. Because the address is part of the hash, a solution seen in the mempool is worthless to anyone else. After each valid solution the challenge becomes the hash of the old challenge, the nonce and the previous block hash, so work cannot be computed ahead.

5.2Reward

With P the tokens held by the mining contract and Δt the seconds since the previous solution, a valid solution earns

r = P · min(Δt, 2,400) / 604,800(5)

At the intended pace of one solution every ten minutes this is 1/1,008 of the pool per solution, which pays out roughly one week's share per week. A miner who floods the contract with solutions seconds apart earns almost nothing for them, and after a long pause no one can sweep up the accumulated pool: one solution earns at most 40 minutes' worth.

5.3Difficulty

After every 144 solutions, about one day at the target pace, the target is rescaled so that solutions arrive every ten minutes on average:

T′ = clamp( T · tactual / (144 · 600 s) , T/4 , 4T )(6)

If fewer than 144 solutions arrive within four days, the target is adjusted early so that mining does not stall when miners leave. The target always stays between 216 and 2240. The easiest setting needs about 65,000 attempts, which an ordinary laptop finds in well under a second; difficulty then rises with participation.

A reference miner for ordinary computers is published with the source code. It is honest to say that dedicated graphics hardware will outpace it as soon as anyone uses such hardware, as in every proof-of-work system.

6Staking

The other half of every mint goes to BcusStaking. Holders deposit BCUS and can withdraw at any time, without a lock-up. Each new amount is streamed evenly over seven days and shared among depositors in proportion to their deposit, using the reward-per-token accounting of the Synthetix staking contract [10]. A deposit made just before a payout therefore earns only the small part streamed while it was in.

Tokens that stream while nobody has deposited anything are burned, by sending them to the address 0x000000000000000000000000000000000000dEaD. Earlier designs kept them for the first depositor, who in practice would have been the founder. Burning removes that advantage.

What a depositor receives depends on the emission, the factor and how much others have deposited. It is not fixed, not guaranteed and not a return promised by anyone. No staking page shows a percentage yield.

7Founder allocation

One million BCUS, 4.8 % of the cap, are minted once at deployment directly into the FounderLock contract, an OpenZeppelin VestingWallet [11]. They become available linearly over ten years from deployment: 100,000 BCUS per year, accrued by the second. Anyone can trigger a release, but the tokens always go to one fixed beneficiary address, which cannot be changed or renounced. There is no cliff, so the market never faces a single large unlock.

There is no pre-sale, no private sale, no team or advisor allocation and no marketing reserve. In the first year the founder's release of 100,000 BCUS compares with about 1.3 million BCUS issued to miners and stakers.

9Security analysis

ThreatEffect and bound
Oracle key stolenFalse but plausible reports. Each report moves issuance by at most 5 %; reaching a limit takes ten weeks of visible, checkable reports. Retirement resets the factor to 1.0.
CDC data delayed or wrongNo report or a rejected report; the factor stays where it is. Grossly wrong values fail the range and ratio checks.
Front-running a mining solutionNot possible: the miner's address is part of the hash.
Burst mining at launch or after a pauseReward is paid by elapsed time and capped at 40 minutes per solution, so speed alone earns nothing extra.
Hash power concentrationOne large miner can win most of the mining share. It cannot affect staking, the oracle or the supply cap.
Flash deposits in stakingSeven-day streaming means a short deposit earns only what streamed while it was in.
Founder dumpingBounded by the vesting schedule to 100,000 BCUS per year.
Contract bugContracts cannot be patched. An independent audit is a condition for mainnet (section 16). The current test suite has 78 tests.

10Ethics of the design

Higher mortality lowers issuance. A fair critic will point out that this makes the token scarcer when more people die. The design does not intend death to be profitable, and it cannot cause or prevent a single death: the factor reads national totals after the fact, eight weeks late, and moves issuance by a few percent at most. The direction was chosen so that the ledger issues less in a year of loss, as a record does not celebrate. The project uses only published national totals and processes no data about any individual.

11Related work

Bitcoin [1] introduced proof of work and the halving schedule that Bloodcoin's issuance follows. Ethereum [2] made general contracts possible, and Base [3] provides the low-fee network BCUS runs on. Ampleforth [4] adjusts supply from an oracle, but its oracle reports its own market price, which ties supply to speculation; Bloodcoin's oracle reports a quantity outside any market. Chainlink [5] provides decentralised oracle networks; Bloodcoin currently uses a single signed oracle and states that limitation in section 8. 0xBitcoin and EIP-918 [9] showed that proof-of-work mining can run inside a token contract.

12The Bloodcoin chain

Before BCUS existed on Base, the project started its own proof-of-work blockchain with SHA-256 mining, a 30-second block time and an explorer at node.bloodcoin-us.com. That chain runs on a single node operated by the publisher, so whoever controls the majority of its computing power controls it. It is an experiment, separate from BCUS on Base, and its coins have no value. A demo exchange trades those coins against test dollars that cannot be paid in or out.

13Environmental impact

Base is a layer-2 network that settles on Ethereum, which uses proof of stake. The proof-of-work search for BCUS runs on the miners' own computers. Because the reward per week is fixed, the energy miners spend is limited by what the reward is worth to them; with little value it stays small. Before any public offer, the publisher will add the energy disclosure that EU and Swiss practice expects for consensus mechanisms [13].

14Risks

  • Total loss. BCUS may never have a value, or may lose all of it.
  • Software. The contracts may contain errors that lead to loss. No independent audit has been completed.
  • Oracle. Issuance depends on one key and on CDC data. Misuse, outages or data changes affect issuance within the stated bounds.
  • Mining concentration. Specialised hardware can dominate mining.
  • Liquidity. There is no market and there may never be one. The publisher will not create one.
  • Regulation. Laws can change and may restrict the project, its use or the transfer of tokens.
  • Reputation and access. Because the project uses mortality data, banks, exchanges and platforms may refuse to work with it.
  • Keys. Lost private keys cannot be recovered by anyone.
  • Tax. Receiving or holding tokens can have tax consequences for the holder.
  • Project. The project is run by one person and can be delayed, changed or stopped.

16Conditions before mainnet

BCUS will be deployed on Base mainnet only when all of the following are met. There is no date.

  1. The legal review in section 15 is complete and its result is followed.
  2. An independent security audit of the contracts is complete and its findings are fixed.
  3. The oracle key is held on a hardware signing device, not on a server.
  4. Several weekly oracle reports and a public mining test have run correctly on the test network.
  5. All public texts are checked so that none implies a value, yield or investment.
  6. The exclusion of persons in the United States is in place.
